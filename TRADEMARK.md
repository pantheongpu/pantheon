# Pantheon trademark policy

Pantheon's source code is open under the AGPL-3.0-only license. That license
covers the code. It does not cover the project's names and logos, which this
policy covers. The reason is simple: people read a result that says "Pantheon" as
a statement that the genuine tool produced it, and we want that to stay true.

In this policy "we" means K Digital Systems LLC, a limited liability company
registered in Texas (the "Company"), which maintains Pantheon.

## What this policy covers

We claim rights in these names and logos (the "Marks"):

- **Pantheon**, when used for GPU testing and diagnostic software
- **PantheonGPU**
- the "P" logo, and any other logo or artwork we publish for the project

Third-party names such as NVIDIA, AMD, CUDA and ROCm belong to their owners.
Pantheon is an independent project and is not endorsed by them.

## What you can do without asking

You may use the name Pantheon, unchanged, to say true things about the project:

- Write about it, review it, teach with it, and link to it.
- Say your software works with, runs, or is tested with Pantheon, or that a
  result came from "Pantheon 1.2.4".
- Distribute unmodified copies, and package it for a Linux distribution or
  package manager under the name the project already uses (such as
  `pantheon-gpu`). Packaging changes that fix build or installation problems and
  do not change what the tool measures, reports or decides count as unmodified.
- Name a user group, meetup or forum about Pantheon, if the name makes clear it
  is unofficial, for example "Pantheon users (unofficial)".
- Use the name inside your own company, on your own GPUs.

When you do, do not suggest that we made, checked or approved what you are doing.

## What needs our written permission

Ask first if you want to:

1. **Name something after it.** This means a product, company, service, domain,
   app, package, social account or event whose name contains a Mark or one that
   is confusingly similar.
2. **Distribute a modified version under the Pantheon name.** If you change
   workloads, thresholds, verdict rules, measurements or reports, call your
   version something else and describe it accurately, for example "Foo, a fork
   of Pantheon". Keep the AGPL notices.
3. **Use the logo.** Logo files are not covered by the AGPL, even where they
   appear in a repository or website that is.
4. **Say your results are certified, verified or endorsed by Pantheon,** or put
   our name on a badge, seal or certificate. We do not run a certification
   program today. If we start one, only reports issued by the Company may use
   words like "certified" or "verified" together with the Pantheon name.
5. **Sell goods or services under a Mark,** for example merchandise.

## Results

A Pantheon report says which version ran and what it measured. Two rules keep
that useful:

- A result from a modified build must not be presented as a Pantheon result
  without saying it comes from a modified build.
- If you sell testing or condition reports, you may say you use Pantheon and
  which version, but the report is yours. Do not word it so that it looks like it
  comes from us.
